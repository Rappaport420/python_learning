- **Two Pointers:** This involves using two indices to traverse an array or linked list, typically starting from opposite ends or moving at different speeds. It is particularly effective for problems involving **pairs, sorted arrays, or linked list cycles**.

- **Sliding Window:** This pattern uses a dynamic window that moves across a sequence (like an array or string), expanding or shrinking based on specific conditions. It is ideal for problems dealing with **continuous subarrays or substrings**.

- **Fast and Slow Pointers:** Similar to two pointers but with the indices moving at different speeds. This technique is commonly used to **detect cycles in linked lists** or to find a middle element.

- **Binary Search Variations:** Beyond the basic version, these modified searches are used for more complex tasks such as **searching in rotated arrays**, finding boundaries, or searching in infinite sequences.

- **Depth First Search (DFS) and Breadth First Search (BFS):** These are essential for navigating **trees and graphs**. DFS explores as deep as possible before checking siblings, while BFS explores the structure level by level.

- **Backtracking:** This pattern explores all possible solutions by building candidates incrementally and abandoning any path ("backtracking") that cannot lead to a valid solution. It is often used for **permutations, combinations**, and constraint satisfaction problems like Sudoku.
